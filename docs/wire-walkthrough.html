The MQTT wire contract, walked through

tanktemp reads DS18B20 probes strapped to hot water cylinders and publishes every sweep to MQTT. This contract was written overnight without your review (G13). This page goes through it in the order a reviewer would: where messages go, when they're sent, what's in them, what goes wrong, and what a consumer does with them. It ends with the choices waiting for your yes or no.

Every payload below is copied byte for byte from the goldens in internal/payload/testdata/, which are the contract. The device names are the made-up ones the goldens use. Where the docs say nothing, this page says so instead of filling the gap.

01 · Where messages go

The topic tree

There are two topics. One carries a state message per tank per sweep. The other carries the daemon's lifecycle and its last will. {prefix} is mqtt.topic_prefix: energy/hot_water by default, test/energy/hot_water for the test deploy until 2026-11-02, when the first real cylinder went live on the default.

{prefix}/{device_id}/state

When
every sweep (default 30 s), one message per tank
QoS
0
Retained
yes
Golden
state-ok, state-failures

QoS 0 is fine here because the next sweep replaces the message within 30 s. Because it's retained, a late subscriber gets the last reading straight away, and its timestamp says how old that reading is.

{prefix}/status

When
STARTUP, first HEARTBEAT (ready), scheduled HEARTBEAT (15 min), HEARTBEAT after every reconnect, SHUTDOWN; the last will
QoS
1 (the will too)
Retained
yes (the will too)
Golden
status-startup, -heartbeat, -shutdown, lwt

This topic belongs to the daemon, not to a tank. So a site has one tanktemp, and a second one would need its own prefix.

Why exactly two subscription patterns

The consumer spec (docs/STATEHOUSE.md) tells adapters to subscribe to {base}/+/state and {base}/status, and not to {base}/#. With the two exact patterns, a topic tanktemp adds later (the dashed branch) never reaches a consumer that wasn't written for it. With #, it would. The + covers any number of tanks. status sits one level higher, so the two patterns can never match the same topic.

02 · When messages go

Lifecycle, over time

From cmd/tanktemp/main.go and common/lifecycle: STARTUP, the first sweep, a ready HEARTBEAT, then state every 30 s and a heartbeat every 15 min, through a broker drop and a SIGTERM. The right-hand column shows what's retained on the broker after each step, which is exactly what a subscriber connecting at that moment would get. Click a row for details.

status (QoS 1, retained) state (QoS 0, retained) last will, published by the broker inside tanktemp, nothing on the wire
Retained says nothing about age. After SHUTDOWN, the retained state still holds the last sweep's reading, indefinitely. That's why consumers have to check timestamp (the 90 s rule in §07), and why the status topic is the one that says whether anyone is publishing.

      04 · Why there's no value
      
  
      Error code reference
      
  
      The codes are part of the contract: internal/reading warns never to rename one. The physical causes come from docs/ONEWIRE.md, a month of rig experiments. Every failed read leaves temp_c and raw_c as null. Every code except not_present is retried, up to sweep.attempts (default 3), because each read is a fresh conversion.
      
  
      
    
      CodeWhat physically happenedPasses kernel CRC?RetriedConsumer should
      
    
      
      not_presentw1_slave is missing (ENOENT): the probe isn't on any bus. It was unplugged, or the kernel dropped it after failed bus searches. If discovery itself fails (no bus masters, for example), every sensor reads this (G11).n/ano (attempts 1)Write nothing. If it lasts N minutes, the probe is down. STATEHOUSE.md leaves N open.
      
      
      bus_lowAll nine scratchpad bytes are 00 and t=0. The data line was held low: the pull-up was lost or DQ was shorted to GND. Because the CRC of all zeros is 0, the kernel says YES and reports a fake 0.000 °C. Seen for real on 2026-09-28 and 2026-10-18.yesyesWrite nothing. This is a wiring fault, not a cold tank.
      
      
      crcThe kernel's verdict was NO, or tanktemp's own CRC check disagreed. Usually noise or a marginal contact. A floating line (all ff, nothing answering) also fails here.noyesWrite nothing. One on its own is transient; if it persists, look at the contact.
      
      
      power_on_resetraw = 0x0550 (85.000 °C) and scratchpad byte 6 = 0x0C. The sensor restarted (it lost 3.3 V) and hasn't converted yet. The pattern comes from the datasheet: a real one has never been captured (G5, synthetic fixture).yesyes; usually clears on the next readWrite nothing. Several probes resetting together points to the power rail.
      
      
      bad_scratchpadThe CRC passed, but the fixed bytes are wrong (byte 4 & 0x9F ≠ 0x1F, byte 5 ≠ ff, or byte 7 ≠ 10). Garbage from a clone, or corrupted data.maybeyesWrite nothing. If it persists, suspect the part.
      
      
      out_of_rangeThe value is outside the DS18B20's −55 to +125 °C range.—yesWrite nothing.
      
      
      malformedThe kernel's w1_slave text didn't have the expected two-line shape.—yesWrite nothing.
      
      
      read_errorAny other failure. Unrecognised errors land here rather than being guessed at, and are retried (G3).—yesWrite nothing.
      
          
  
      
  
      Not an error: suspect: "85c_unverified". 85 °C is the sensor's power-on value, but a cylinder can genuinely reach it. Any 85.000 that passes every check gets one extra read, outside the attempt budget (G2). If that read gives a different good value, the new value replaces the 85. Otherwise the 85 is published with the flag. Consumers should write the value, and keep the flag if they can carry per-reading quality.
      
  
      The other sentinel, internal/reading's cancelled, is never published: a sweep cancelled by shutdown is thrown away.
      

      



      
  
      05 · The daemon's own topic
      
  
      Status messages
      
  
      All four goldens, side by side. HEARTBEAT has no separate "ready" or "reconnect" event: the first HEARTBEAT after the first sweep is the ready signal, and the HEARTBEAT republished after a reconnect (G19) looks just like a scheduled one.
      
  
      
  
      Field by field
      
  
      
  
      "absent" means the key isn't in the message. Apart from reason, which appears on SHUTDOWN only, a key is missing only in the last will, which carries just two keys.
      

  
      startup_problems
      
  
      These come from the startup checks, and each one has a rom (null for a bus-wide problem), a stable code and a human message (G15). The codes are discover_failed, not_present, parasite_power, resolution and inspect_failed. Consumers should match on code, never on the message. The STARTUP golden has both kinds: a parasite-powered probe, and "rom":null with "no 1-Wire bus masters".
      
  
      Health isn't on the wire. The status message doesn't carry a health verdict. Health is /healthz, decided in internal/status. It is starting before the first sweep, and degraded on any of: MQTT disconnected, any probe with an error, the last sweep older than 3 × interval (90 s, G17), clock not synced, discovery failed, or any startup problem. Decided change (G16): a suspect 85 °C reading will now degrade health too. The current code still treats it as "not a fault". It doesn't change the wire.

      c · Timing: sweep_ms against the 30 s interval
      
  
      sweep_ms is the whole sweep, across all tanks. Buses are read in parallel, and reads on one bus are serialised by the kernel at about 0.8 s each. If a sweep is still running when the next tick comes, that tick is skipped, not queued (G6).
      
  

      



      
  
      07 · What statehouse does with it
      
  
      Consumer mapping
      
  
      From docs/STATEHOUSE.md, which is a spec, not yet implemented. The engine device is a probe, not a tank: scheme = "tanktemp", primary = "<device_id>.<position>", for example hot-water-cylinder.top. The identity is the position, not the ROM, so replacing a probe keeps its series going.

      errorthe reason; match on the codes, which are stable

      sweep_msdiagnostics
      
      
      status SHUTDOWN / LWTmark every tanktemp device offline
      
      
      sensors_ok, sensors_failed, unexpected, startup_problemspublisher health; may show on a status page; never create devices from them
      
          
  
      
  
      Retained replay. On every (re)connect the broker hands statehouse the last state of every cylinder at once. Accept a retained state as current only if its timestamp is within 3 sweep intervals (90 s) of now. Otherwise mark those devices stale, or wait for the next live message. Never write the same reading twice: tanktemp republishes every 30 s, so a timestamp you've already seen means a replay.
      

      



      
  
      08 · How it may change
      
  
      Compatibility rules
      
  
      
    
      
      
      Additive (minor)
      
      
        
        
      • A new field. Adapters must ignore fields they don't know.
        • 
        
      • The goldens change too, since they pin bytes and key order. The change starts in WIRE.md.
        • 
      
      
    
      
    
      
      
      Breaking (major), announced in WIRE.md first
      
      
        
        
      • Renaming or removing a field.
        • 
        
      • Changing an error code.
        • 
        
      • The prefix moved from test/energy/hot_water to energy/hot_water on 2026-11-02. Adapters keep the base configurable.
        • 
      
      
    
      
  
      
  
      The goldens are the contract. internal/payload/testdata/*.golden pin the exact bytes, and a diff there is a breaking change. Never edit a golden to make a test pass. STATEHOUSE.md offers the same files to the adapter as test fixtures.
      
  
      Where the docs are silent: they don't say whether adding a new error code is additive or major (they only cover changing one). They don't say what happens to a value's type, such as an int becoming a float. And the payloads carry no schema or contract version field, so a consumer can only tell versions apart by version, the build commit.
      

      



      
  
      09 · Found while building this page
      
  
      Where WIRE.md and the goldens or code disagreed
      
  
      All ten were resolved on 2026-11-01; each says how. wire changes bytes on the wire. doc is wording. silent means the contract doesn't say.
      
  
        
    
      1. raw_c isn't a multiple of 0.0625 in practice.wire
        WIRE.md says raw_c is "a multiple of 0.0625", and G10 says it's "exact". But the real Source converts the scratchpad the way the kernel's t= does, with integer millidegrees: Scratchpad.MilliC() = raw*1000/16, and Celsius() then divides by 1000. So 12.0625 °C publishes as raw_c: 12.062, and temp_c is calibrated from that. The state-ok golden's 12.0625 comes from a hand-built test input that the Source can't produce. The sample CSVs (20.562, 20.187) show the truncated form. A value like 61.375 is exact either way.
        Fixed: The code was wrong, not the contract. sweep now rounds the kernel's millidegrees back to the exact sixteenth (TestRawIsExactSixteenths covers every value from −55 to +125 °C), so 12.0625 publishes as 12.0625. The goldens didn't change.
        2. 
    
      2. The topics table leaves out the reconnect HEARTBEAT.doc
        WIRE.md's table lists STARTUP, first HEARTBEAT, scheduled HEARTBEAT and SHUTDOWN. main.go also republishes a HEARTBEAT after every reconnect (G19), and STATEHOUSE.md says so.
        Fixed: WIRE.md's table now lists it.
        3. 
    
      3. A HEARTBEAT can carry ready:false.doc
        A reconnect before the first sweep has finished republishes the status as HEARTBEAT, and ready is still false. STATEHOUSE.md's status table presents HEARTBEAT as "ready":true.
        Fixed: WIRE.md's ready row now says so.
        4. 
    
      4. ready means "swept", not "published".doc
        WIRE.md says ready is true "once the first complete sweep has been published". The tracker sets it when the sweep is recorded, before the state publish, so a first sweep whose publishes all failed still makes ready true.
        Fixed: WIRE.md now describes what the code does: true once the first sweep is taken, even if its publish was lost.
        5. 
    
      5. "Passed every check and a second read" is stronger than the code.doc
        In recheck85, the 85 is kept and flagged if the extra read fails for any reason (a CRC error, say), not only when it reads 85 again. So a suspect 85 hasn't necessarily been confirmed by a second good read. When the extra read gives a different good value, that value replaces the 85 with no flag, and attempts shows the extra read.
        Fixed: WIRE.md's suspect row now says the extra read either read 85 again or failed.
        6. 
    
      6. STARTUP isn't guaranteed to arrive.silent
        If the broker isn't connected within 10 s (G18), the STARTUP publish fails and isn't retried. The first status a consumer sees might then be the ready HEARTBEAT. WIRE.md doesn't mention this.
        Fixed: Covered in WIRE.md's new Delivery section.
        7. 
    
      7. State published while disconnected is dropped, not queued.silent
        mqttpub fails a publish at once when the connection is down. After a reconnect, only the status is republished, and the state catches up at the next sweep (≤ 30 s later). This is correct, but WIRE.md doesn't say it.
        Fixed: Covered in WIRE.md's new Delivery section.
        8. 
    
      8. A failed SHUTDOWN becomes an LWT.silent
        mqttpub.Close disconnects cleanly only if the last publish got through. Otherwise it leaves without a DISCONNECT, so the broker publishes the will. WIRE.md describes the will only as "if tanktemp vanishes".
        Fixed: Covered in WIRE.md's new Delivery section.
        9. 
    
      9. The worked example's recovery sweep isn't a CSV row.doc
        STATEHOUSE.md's sweep 4 (20.5 / 20.25 / 20.187) matches the test replay, not any single row of fault-data-line.csv. The first recovered row is 20.562 / 20.250 / 20.187. That's harmless, but the example says it "actually happened".
        Fixed: STATEHOUSE.md now uses the CSV's real rows (16:51 and 17:02:30) as exact sixteenths.
        10. 
    
      10. G16 in GUESSES.md vs the decision.doc
        G16 and the Health comment still say a suspect reading doesn't degrade health. You've decided it will. That's a health change and doesn't touch the wire, but both texts will need updating along with the code.
        Fixed: The code, the comment and GUESSES.md now match the decision.
