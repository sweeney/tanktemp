tanktemp · hot-water meter · design explainer (with washhouse)

The hot-water meter

A pulsed water meter on the pipe where the two cylinders' hot outlets join. It ticks once per litre. tanktemp counts the ticks and publishes them, and washhouse turns them into draws that are measured, not estimated. The probes keep the jobs only they can do: which tank gave the water, how hot it was, and how much is left.

1 · The meter

One pulse per litre

A Watersavers WMP20H, K=1: a ¾" multi-jet meter with a hot-water body and a two-core pulse cable. Inside, a magnet on the 1-litre pointer closes a switch once per turn. That makes it simple to read, but coarse in time. At shower flows a pulse comes every 7–10 s, and a dribbling basin tap gives one every half minute.

2 · Where it goes

On the combined hot outlet

The meter sits after the two B outlets merge, so it sees every litre that leaves the cylinders as hot water. It sees the tanks together, so washhouse splits each draw by tank using the probes. The right tank's secondary return is the unknown. The owner doesn't expect the loop to draw from these outlet pipes, and washhouse is built to check that rather than assume it.

Loop:
Expected: the meter sees draws only. Overnight it is silent, and every pulse belongs to a tap. Volumes are exact to a litre, plus 3 %. Thermal expansion doesn't pass through it either: on a reheat the extra volume goes back towards the expansion vessel on the cold side.
The check, if the loop did share it. A thermosiphon of about 0.3 L/min would tick the meter every 3–4 minutes, all night, with probe 1 flat (no cold water enters). washhouse would see that pattern, learn it as a baseline and subtract it. Then the meter becomes the first direct measurement of the loop. Try it with the "loop shares the outlet" switch in the simulator. If it happens, the cold feed is the fallback position for the meter.
Real data · the first night, 19–20 Nov 2026

What the installed meter actually did

The meter went in on 19 Nov. tanktemp's session recorded every raw edge at the pin for 15½ hours (gpiomon, no debounce, the Pi's internal pull-up only) alongside the boiler events. This section replays that recording, all 277 edges of it, through the counters and the draw rules. Nothing here is simulated. Times are UTC, which is UK time in November.

What it showed

  • The reed is clean. Every real close and open is a single edge, with no mechanical bounce. The pointer rested on the magnet for 4.4 h with no chatter.
  • The noise is electrical. 15 edges in 8 clusters are spikes of a few microseconds, most within a second of the programmer switching. The worst was 8 edges at HW off. Each one reports the level the line already had, so none changes the state.
  • Flows are faster than the simulation assumed. The bath ran at 32–39 L/min, a pulse every 1.6–1.9 s. The shower ran at 7 L/min and the sinks at 4–5 L/min.
  • Silent overnight from 23:02 to 07:37. That fits "no loop through the meter", but the meter's exact plumbing position hasn't been confirmed yet.
  • The closed time is a speedometer. The reed stays closed for about 23 % of each turn. So when it opens, the flow is known about 2 s into a shower, without waiting for the second litre.

What it changes

  • tanktemp watches both edges and counts real state changes: open, then closed, is one litre. It never trusts the edge labels.
  • The 20 ms kernel debounce is mandatory, and config refuses 0. The 600 ms minimum gap stays as a backstop. Without the debounce, the HW-off burst alone counts 2 phantom litres, which pass as a draw.
  • tanktemp publishes noise (rejected spikes) apart from glitches, plus last_closed_ms. washhouse ties the noise to boiler events, since tanktemp doesn't subscribe to them.
  • Kind by flow: 20 L/min or more is a bath. Volume alone would call a 23 L bath top-up a tap.
  • An input board (10 kΩ pull-up, 10 kΩ series, 100 nF) is specified but not fitted. Connect the meter leads only with the Pi off: the last Pi died when they were pushed on live.

5 · Draws: measured vs estimated

  
Every draw in the day, three ways

  
The meter counts hot litres. A mixer shower takes only about 60–70 % of its flow from the cylinder, and the rest is cold. To compare with washhouse's heat balance, which counts in litres of 40 °C water, the metered litres are converted using the outlet temperature from the probes: L × (Tout − 10) ÷ 30. Click a row to jump there.

  

  
The detection speed is about even: the probes often trigger on the first sweep after a tap opens, and the meter needs its second litre. The meter's gain is certainty and size: every draw gets a measured volume, including the two that a reheat hides from the heat balance.

6 · Calibration

  
Every clean draw is a free test of the model

  

    

      
Heat balance against meter · clean draws only (no reheat), litres at 40 °C

      
    

    

      
washhouse's thermal model is built on guesses: the tank's inner diameter, the depth of its domes, and a straight line between probes. Until now nothing could check it. With the meter, every draw not masked by a reheat gives a pair: what the heat balance said and what actually came out. washhouse records each pair (washhouse_calibration in Influx) and shows the running median and spread on /api/v1/model.

      
The simulated day shows why that matters: the errors are not one constant factor. Small draws come out high, because the probes' straight-line profile can't see a thin cold layer. Big draws can be high or low, depending on how stratified the tank was. One geometry tweak won't fix that. The meter gives a fortnight of pairs to see which model change does, and in the meantime the published volumes are simply right.

      

    

  







  
7 · On the wire

  
What tanktemp adds, and what washhouse adds

  
tanktemp publishes raw facts only: a counter and when the last pulse came. Flow rates and draws belong downstream, as its WIRE.md already says. Topics (decided 20 Nov 2026): everything about hot water lives under water/hot/. tanktemp owns water/hot/tanktemp/…, its raw facts, having moved from energy/hot_water. washhouse owns the four named topics above it. The meter topic deliberately doesn't match +/state, which statehouse's tanktemp adapter subscribes to, so that adapter never mistakes it for a cylinder.

8 · Plan

  
How it gets built

  

    

      
1
Fitted 19 Nov; finish the checks. It's on GPIO 27 (pin 13) with the internal pull-up, and one night is recorded. Still to do: confirm where on the pipework it sits and where the loop's flow leg starts; multimeter the reed against the pipe; read the cable label and length; fit the input board. Only ever connect it with the Pi off.

      
2
tanktemp, in its own repo and under its rules. WIRE.md and goldens first. Then a pure internal/meter counter (added to boundaries_test.go), and internal/gpioedge as the only go-gpiocdev code: both edges into a state machine (a litre is open then closed; labels are never trusted), a mandatory 20 ms kernel debounce, monotonic edge times, and LineSeqno gaps counted as lost. The unit needs /dev/gpiochip0: today PrivateDevices=true hides it, so either drop that or add DeviceAllow + the gpio group. That's a hardening trade-off for the owner (a separate meter daemon would avoid it). A fake and a contract test, config, health, and a pulsewatch rig tool. Counting runs on its own goroutine, because a probe sweep blocks for 4–8 s. Then, in a separate PR, the first pulse switches the probe sweeps to fast.

      
3
Fit it, then do a bucket test. Fitted vertically on 19 Nov. The test is deferred by the owner. Draw 10 L into a measured bucket three times, and compare the pulses, the published count and the dial. Switch the boiler programme on and off during the run to prove the debounce live. Repeat per outlet, which also gives the flow rates the briefing lacks. A read-only script with --check, tested first.

      
4
Record a week as the first metered fixtures, using the main plan's PII scrubbing.

      
5
devices_home entry hot_water_meter, class flow_meter (not occupancy-relevant), with a meter block that includes placement. common/devices ignores unknown keys, so it's safe today.

      
6
washhouse. wire/tanktemp Meter type; ingest; flow; the metered detector alongside the thermal one; energy, split by tank and calibration; the cross-checks; outputs. Since washhouse doesn't exist yet, this is designed in from the start.

    

    

      
Rules washhouse applies (starting values; all in config)

      

        
StepRule

        
StartThe second pulse within 36 s (faster than the vertical Q1 of 1.67 L/min, and well under a third of any circulation interval). started_at = first pulse − ½ gap. A lone pulse is never a draw by itself.

        
EndNo pulse for 4 × the median gap, kept between 30 and 120 s. ended_at = last pulse + ½ gap.

        
VolumePulses × 1 L (minus the circulation baseline, if one was ever detected). ±(1 L + 3 %).

        
EnergyFor each litre: (Tout − Tcold) × 4.186 kJ. Tout is the hotter of P3 and P5 per tank, averaged over the two tanks.

        
SplitEach tank's fall in stored heat after loss. During a reheat, the learnt split from clean draws.

        
KindFlow first: 20 L/min or more is a bath (the real bath ran at 32–39 L/min, the shower at 7). Otherwise by hot litres: tap under 15 L; bath 80 L and over; shower in between when it lasts ≥ 3 min. A hint, tuned from real draws.

        
Flow earlyFrom last_closed_ms: flow ≈ 60 × duty ÷ closed seconds, with the duty (about 0.23) learnt from steady draws. A flow figure arrives about 1.5 s into a shower rather than at the second litre.

        
Cross-checkThe probes see a big draw (inlet drop ≥ 8 °C) with no pulses → meter_suspect, and volumes fall back to the heat balance.

        
statehouseNo change. Metered draws come at 0.95 confidence, so the existing gate (≥ 0.7, ≥ 0.5 L) passes them, including the reheat-masked ones it used to drop.
